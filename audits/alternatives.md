# Alternatives

This method is not the only way to structure agentic product engineering.

## Lean Prototype First

Build a small prototype immediately, then learn from use.

Best when:

- risk is low;
- the product is simple;
- discovery is cheap;
- no external side effects exist.

Tradeoff:

- can under-translate ambition and create misleading success.

## Traditional Product Requirements Document

Write a complete PRD before execution.

Best when:

- teams need formal approval;
- scope is stable;
- stakeholders require documentation.

Tradeoff:

- can become slow and detached from implementation evidence.

## Human-Led Architecture First

An architect defines the system before involving agents.

Best when:

- domain risk is high;
- safety or compliance matters;
- agent autonomy is not yet trusted.

Tradeoff:

- reduces agentic speed and may miss ordinary-language feedback loops.

## Tool-Specific Agent Workflow

Use one tool's native workflow, such as a Codex-only or Hermes-only process.

Best when:

- the team uses one environment;
- portability is not important.

Tradeoff:

- creates lock-in and may hide method concepts inside tool behavior.

## Why This Method Exists

The Autonomous Product Engineering Method aims for a middle path: structured enough to prevent shallow or unsafe autonomy, but practical enough to support real execution across tools.
